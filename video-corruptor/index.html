Rendering plays the video through the effects in real time, so a 1-minute clip takes about a minute (less if you sped it up). Keep this tab visible while it runs.

This is the real datamosh trick glitch artists use. A video only stores a full picture every second or two (a keyframe). Every frame in between just says how the last picture moves. This removes the keyframes, so the old picture never gets replaced. Instead it gets dragged, stretched and melted by the motion of whatever comes next.

Works best on MP4 / MOV files using H.264, which most videos use. H.265/HEVC files (iPhone "High Efficiency") also work, but most players fill in the missing pictures with grey, so you get ghostly outlines instead of the colourful smear. For the full effect on those, use the 🎨 Glitch tab. The very first frame is kept, so the start looks normal and the mush builds from there. It looks best on videos with movement and scene changes. The result often plays more reliably in VLC than in a browser. To datamosh any other format, run it through 🎨 Glitch first with the Datamosh sliders up, or convert it to MP4 first.

This damages the actual bytes inside the file, the way a dying hard drive would. The video decoder then gets confused and makes grey smears, rainbow blocks and frames that melt into each other. Every click gives a different result.

Tip: MP4 and AVI files work best. Corrupted files often play better in VLC or mpv than in a browser, and different players show the damage differently. Browsers sometimes refuse to play them at all.

No uploads, no servers. It's just your browser and a lot of bad decisions.
